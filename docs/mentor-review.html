Concept 1: Consolidated learning direction

School-Guided Learning Support

More than an AI tutor: a human-supervised learning and accountability loop connecting the learner, authorized parent, and teacher.

Shared problem

General AI may operate outside the teacher's curriculum, reveal answers instead of teaching, and provide little appropriate visibility into how the learner progressed. Students may also struggle to organize preparation or find suitable academic support. The opportunity is to connect teacher-approved assignments, lesson reviews, and mock quizzes to useful follow-up without continuous monitoring or surveillance.

Three team contributions being consolidated

Detailed public brief

Personalized Study Buddy

Reviews a practice attempt, chooses a teacher-approved hint, checks a fresh question, and adjusts within limits. A no-hint check then gives each authorized participant an appropriate next step: the learner continues practicing, the parent supports routines and encouragement, and the teacher decides whether follow-up is needed.

Teacher's Assistant AI

Lets a teacher set an AI-use level and upload a rubric. Three proposed agents coach without rewriting, record assignment-specific drafts and AI interactions, and flag process-to-final-work differences for teacher review. The teacher makes every final decision.

Small four-week demonstration

Teacher assigns an approved activity
Student completes guided practice
No-hint progress check
Accomplishment level updates
Student-parent-teacher next step

What the demo proves

The workflow follows approved learning boundaries, changes support after a checked result, and updates a clear level: assigned, started, guided practice completed, independent check completed, or teacher reviewed. The learner, parent, and teacher receive only the progress information and next step appropriate to their role. Completion does not automatically mean mastery.

Keep outside the first prototype

Tutor marketplace, payments, campus forecasting, complete parent and teacher portals, misconduct judgments, class analytics, and unnecessary multi-agent complexity.

Concept 2: Family-school direction

Family-School Communication Coordinator

Help families understand approved school information while keeping educators responsible for unresolved or sensitive questions.

Problem

Families may receive information through separate notices, emails, messages, calendars, and verbal updates. Busy parents can miss changes or encounter incomplete or apparently conflicting details, while educators repeat information or correct misunderstandings.

Small four-week demonstration

Parent asks about conflicting notices
Permission is checked
Approved sources are compared
Agent explains or requests clarification
Educator confirms the answer

Proposed response

Retrieve only approved information, compare sources and dates, provide a cited answer when evidence is clear, and ask an authorized educator when it is not. The agent never guesses.

Review full Family-School details

Keep outside the first prototype

Replacing existing school systems, unsupervised mass messaging, real student records, real family data, and simultaneous integration with every communication channel.

Why this is a strong four-week candidate

Documented problem context

Published evidence and lived school-community experience support fragmented communication, platform overload, unclear information, and response pressure. This validates the problem context, not demand for our exact solution.

Clear problem-to-solution fit

The agent retrieves approved information, reconciles sources, explains what is confirmed, and asks an educator when evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

Small, visible demonstration

One parent question, two conflicting notices, one clarification request, and one educator-confirmed answer form a complete end-to-end story.

Practical build scope

Fictional notices and fixed permissions can demonstrate the workflow without real student records, school integrations, or a replacement messaging platform.

Defensible agentic behavior

The next action depends on the retrieved evidence: answer, identify uncertainty, or request human clarification. Fixed application rules control access and privacy.

Clear four-week delivery path

The team can build and test the core communication loop first, then treat broader integrations and communication channels as future possibilities.

Evidence boundary: Existing research supports the underlying communication problem. The team still needs current parent and educator feedback to establish frequency, priority, adoption, and whether this workflow is preferable to improving existing tools.
