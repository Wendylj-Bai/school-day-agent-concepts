Concept 1 | Mentor's five pointsPost-award operations

Problem: A university wins the award. The researcher still cannot spend.

1Problem

Award-setup specialists manually read and re-key varied award terms, then chase prerequisites, delaying account creation.

2Target user

A post-award setup specialist in a university Sponsored Programs Office.

3Breaking point

A non-standard document must become accurate system fields, prerequisites, owners, and follow-ups.

4Evidence

Kentucky publishes an 8–18 business-day guide. Michigan State reports only about 53% within one month.

5Success metric

Median business days from non-federal award receipt to account number issued.

4 / 9 | Sources: University of Kentucky lead-times guide; Michigan State OSP/CGA metrics. Michigan State reported 1,367 awards in its cited period.

Proposed solution: Convert each award into a verified action plan.

Agent contribution

Extract terms with page citations, flag uncertainty, create a prerequisite checklist, assign responsible offices, and draft internal follow-ups.

Competition and opening

Huron, Kuali, Cayuse, InfoEd, Workday, and university-built tools already serve this market. Our residual hypothesis is non-federal and amendment documents plus prerequisite coordination.

5 / 9 | Kentucky, Michigan, and the University of Idaho-linked AI4RA project show that award-document extraction is already being explored. We do not claim an empty market.
Concept 2 | Mentor's five pointsPre-award operations

Problem: A strong proposal can lose before anyone reviews the research.

1Problem

Pre-award specialists manually check late proposal drafts against sponsor-specific instructions; a miss can prevent review.

2Target user

A university pre-award grants specialist responsible for institutional review.

3Breaking point

The final narrative must be compared with rules scattered across a long solicitation under deadline pressure.

4Evidence

NSF can return noncompliant proposals without review. Current platforms already perform many structural checks.

5Success metric

Sponsor-specific deficiencies caught before submission per 10 proposals.

6 / 9 | Source: NSF Proposal & Award Policies and Procedures Guide. Frequency of narrative misses remains unknown and must be measured.
Concept 2 | Current journeyA second reader, not a grant writer

Proposed solution: A second reader for opportunity-specific fine print.

1Sponsor publishes solicitation
2Researcher prepares proposal
Break AExtract special requirements
Break BFind evidence in the final draft
5Return corrections
6Human authorizes submission
Agent contribution

Build a source-cited requirement checklist, match each requirement to proposal evidence, flag gaps and conflicts, and escalate uncertainty.

Competition and opening

Cayuse, Kuali, Huron, InfoEd, Grants.gov, and Research.gov already validate much of the submission. The residual hypothesis is program-specific narrative requirements beyond form checks.

RecommendationChoose what to validate, not what to overclaim

Start with the Research Award Setup Coordinator.

Why it leads

A recurring operational workflow, a visible elapsed-time metric, and a clear coordination problem after the document is read.

Main risk: waiting on other offices may dominate the delay, and software may not change their behavior.

Cheapest credible test

At 2–3 universities, examine the last 20 award setups. Record sponsor type, receipt date, account date, active staff minutes, prerequisites, follow-ups, and waiting time by office.

Then test Concept 2 on the last 20 proposals by counting special-rule defects and review minutes.

Our claim today: documented problems, bounded concepts, and measurable hypotheses.

9 / 9 | We are not claiming validated demand, willingness to pay, or a proven performance improvement. Those require direct institutional evidence.
