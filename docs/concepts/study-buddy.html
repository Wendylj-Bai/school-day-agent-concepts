Concept 02 · Adaptive learning with human support

Personalized Study Buddy

Help a learner move forward after a practice mistake while keeping parents, teachers, and advisers involved in encouragement, content guidance, and follow-up.

Recurring practice needTwo bounded agent rolesParents and educators remain involved
Working hypothesis for team discussion. The technology supports learning coordination; it does not replace a teacher, detach parents from the learner, diagnose a child, or prove learning impact.
Problem and pain

A learner can remain stuck when timely, appropriate help is unavailable.

A fictional learner answers ½ + ¼ as ²⁄₆. A static answer key reveals the result but cannot explain the misunderstanding. A generic chatbot may simply provide the homework answer. This is a plausible recurring situation, not measured evidence of learning demand.

Core problem

The learner needs help at the moment of difficulty

Students may struggle outside school hours when a teacher or a parent with subject knowledge is not immediately available.

Pain experienced

Repeated mistakes can become frustration

A generic explanation may not address the misunderstanding. Busy parents may want to help but may not know which explanation to try, while teachers cannot provide individual support at every moment.

Agentic response

Interpret, teach, check, and adapt within limits

The Learning-State Agent identifies a possible misconception from practice attempts. The Teaching Agent selects a vetted hint or activity, presents a fresh question, and changes strategy when the deterministic check shows that the learner is still struggling.

Why agents

The next step depends on what just happened

The workflow must observe an attempt, choose an appropriate strategy, check whether it worked, adapt within a retry limit, and involve an authorized adult when needed. A fixed quiz cannot perform that complete learning loop.

Value for each participant

Student value

Receive a hint matched to the attempt, practice independently, and ask for help without being handed the answer.

Parent value

See a concise, authorized progress and support summary, then encourage practice or help arrange follow-up.

Teacher and adviser value

Approve learning activities, guide lesson review, and receive a useful signal when persistent difficulty needs human attention.

Proposed agent design

Separate interpretation from teaching strategy, then reconnect the learner with people.

The two roles create an observable learning loop while deterministic code retains control of correctness, limits, and authorized sharing.

Agent 1

Learning-State Agent

Reviews the current and recent practice attempts, records a possible misconception with supporting evidence, and identifies the next learning objective. It does not diagnose or grade the learner.

Agent 2

Teaching Agent

Selects a teacher-vetted activity or visual, gives a hint rather than the answer, and presents a different practice question. After an unsuccessful check, it selects another bounded strategy.

Deterministic controls

Correctness, limits, and sharing are not model opinions.

check_practice_answer assesses the fresh response. Code controls retries, session length, allowed content, answer leakage, and what progress information each adult may see.

Human participation

The loop ends with encouragement or appropriate follow-up.

Parents can encourage the learner and help with study routines. Teachers or advisers approve content and guide lesson review. Persistent difficulty produces a support signal, not an autonomous diagnosis.

Illustrative seven-minute demo

Show adaptation and participation, not an isolated chatbot.

  1. A fictional student makes a common mistake in an equivalent-fractions practice exercise.
  2. The deterministic checker marks the attempt incorrect; the Learning-State Agent records a possible misconception.
  3. The Teaching Agent calls a teacher-approved activity tool and presents a fraction-bar hint plus a fresh check question.
  4. The student still struggles, so the workflow records the failed strategy and selects a number-line approach within the retry limit.
  5. The transfer check produces a concise support summary. A parent can encourage review, while the teacher or adviser can recommend the appropriate next lesson.

Suggested MVP · not implemented

One skill, a small vetted library, and one support loop.

One narrow skill

Equivalent fractions, planted mistake patterns, fictional student profiles, fresh check questions, and one authorized parent or educator view.

Suggested stack

LangGraph for workflow state; MCP tools for approved activities and hints; deterministic checking; Base44 or a custom interface; Antigravity for development.

Measures

Transfer to a fresh question, answer leakage, appropriate strategy changes, retry compliance, useful support signals, and timely human follow-up.
