Concept 02 · Adaptive learning with human support

Personalized Study Buddy

Help a learner move forward after a practice mistake while keeping parents, teachers, and advisers involved in encouragement, content guidance, and follow-up.

Recurring practice needTwo bounded agent rolesParents and educators remain involved
Working hypothesis for team discussion. The technology supports learning coordination; it does not replace a teacher, detach parents from the learner, diagnose a child, or prove learning impact.
Problem and pain

A learner can remain stuck when timely, appropriate help is unavailable.

A fictional learner answers ½ + ¼ as ²⁄₆. A static answer key reveals the result but cannot explain the misunderstanding. A generic chatbot may simply provide the homework answer. This is a plausible recurring situation, not measured evidence of learning demand.

Core problem

The learner needs help at the moment of difficulty

Students may struggle outside school hours when a teacher or a parent with subject knowledge is not immediately available.

Pain experienced

Repeated mistakes can become frustration

A generic explanation may not address the misunderstanding. Busy parents may want to help but may not know which explanation to try, while teachers cannot provide individual support at every moment.

Agentic response

Interpret, teach, check, and adapt within limits

The Learning-State Agent identifies a possible misconception from practice attempts. The Teaching Agent selects a vetted hint or activity, presents a fresh question, and changes strategy when the deterministic check shows that the learner is still struggling.

Why agents

The next step depends on what just happened

The workflow must observe an attempt, choose an appropriate strategy, check whether it worked, adapt within a retry limit, and involve an authorized adult when needed. A fixed quiz cannot perform that complete learning loop.

Value for each participant

Student value

Receive a hint matched to the attempt, practice independently, and ask for help without being handed the answer.

Parent value

See a concise, authorized progress and support summary, then encourage practice or help arrange follow-up.

Teacher and adviser value

Approve learning activities, guide lesson review, and receive a useful signal when persistent difficulty needs human attention.

Proposed agent design

Separate interpretation from teaching strategy, then reconnect the learner with people.

The two roles create an observable learning loop while deterministic code retains control of correctness, limits, and authorized sharing.

Agent 1

Learning-State Agent

Reviews the current and recent practice attempts, records a possible misconception with supporting evidence, and identifies the next learning objective. It does not diagnose or grade the learner.

Agent 2

Teaching Agent

Selects a teacher-vetted activity or visual, gives a hint rather than the answer, and presents a different practice question. After an unsuccessful check, it selects another bounded strategy.

Deterministic controls

Correctness, limits, and sharing are not model opinions.

check_practice_answer assesses the fresh response. Code controls retries, session length, allowed content, answer leakage, and what progress information each adult may see.

Human participation

The loop ends with encouragement or appropriate follow-up.

Parents can encourage the learner and help with study routines. Teachers or advisers approve content and guide lesson review. Persistent difficulty produces a support signal, not an autonomous diagnosis.

Practice and mock-test mode

Prepare independently, then adapt the next practice plan.

The Study Buddy can create a short quiz or mock test from a teacher-approved question bank. During the attempt, hints and answers stay locked so the result reflects independent practice rather than assisted performance.

Before the mock test

The learner selects an approved skill and difficulty. The system creates fresh practice questions without copying a real assessment.

During the attempt

No hints, answer reveals, or strategy coaching. Deterministic code records and scores objective responses after submission.

After submission

The agents explain missed concepts using approved material, recommend focused practice, and create a fresh follow-up quiz. The result is a practice-readiness summary, not an official grade or predicted test score.

Preparation and formative practice only. The system must not assist during a live, graded, or restricted assessment.

How to read this flow

A practice mistake becomes a bounded learning and support loop.

The agents help choose an explanation and next step. Ordinary code still controls correctness, retry limits, approved content, and who may see the learner’s support summary.

  1. The learner attempts a practice question. The system begins with what the learner actually tried, not with a general request for the homework answer.
  2. A deterministic checker marks the response. Code decides whether the fresh practice answer is correct; the language model does not invent the result.
  3. The Learning-State Agent forms a limited hypothesis. It records a possible misconception supported by the attempts. It does not diagnose the learner or assign an unofficial grade.
  4. The Teaching Agent selects a vetted way to help. It provides a hint or activity and then asks a different practice question. A short no-hint mock quiz checks independent transfer.
  5. The workflow adapts or involves people. If the learner needs more practice, the Strategy Retry step begins a new bounded pass with a different approved method. After the retry limit, it prepares a concise summary for an authorized parent, teacher, or adviser. The adult decides what encouragement, review, or follow-up is appropriate.
Illustrative seven-minute demo

Show adaptation and participation, not an isolated chatbot.

  1. A fictional student makes a common mistake while adding fractions with unlike denominators.
  2. The deterministic checker marks the attempt incorrect; the Learning-State Agent records a possible misconception.
  3. The Teaching Agent calls a teacher-approved activity tool and presents a fraction-bar hint plus a fresh check question.
  4. The student still struggles, so the workflow records the failed strategy and selects a number-line approach within the retry limit.
  5. A short no-hint mock quiz checks independent transfer. After submission, the system produces a concise support summary for parent encouragement and educator follow-up.
Research validation and limits

The learning need is documented; this tutor’s learning benefit is not.

Tutoring is an evidence-supported intervention category, but no measured benefit exists yet for this product, its interpretation of learner mistakes, or its two-agent design.

What the evidence supports

J-PAL’s review of experimental PreK–12 tutoring studies reported an overall pooled effect of 0.37 standard deviations. That result belongs to the tutoring programs studied; it is not an expected effect size for an AI tutor.

Why independent checks matter

In a Penn/Wharton experiment with nearly 1,000 high-school mathematics students in Turkey, general GPT improved assisted practice but students performed 17% worse than the control group when AI was removed. A guarded tutor mitigated that harm but did not outperform the control group on the independent exam.

Smallest credible test

Compare one adaptive fractions workflow with a fixed vetted explanation. Use different but comparable questions before support, immediately afterward, and later without hints; measure fresh-question accuracy, retention, answer leakage, and appropriate strategy changes.

Finding that would weaken it

Independent performance does not improve over the fixed explanation, the agent repeatedly misreads learner reasoning, or the experience encourages answer dependence.

Content correction: 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/6 is a fraction-addition error, not automatically an equivalent-fractions error. Ask about the learner’s reasoning before labeling a misconception.

Sources: J-PAL, 2020 · Knowledge at Wharton, 2024

Suggested MVP · not implemented

One skill, a small vetted library, and one support loop.

One narrow skill

Adding fractions with unlike denominators, planted mistake patterns, fictional student profiles, fresh check questions, a short mock quiz, and one authorized parent or educator view.

Suggested stack

LangGraph for workflow state; MCP tools for approved activities and hints; deterministic checking; Base44 or a custom interface; Antigravity for development.

Measures

Transfer to fresh post-hint and no-hint mock questions, answer leakage, appropriate strategy changes, retry compliance, useful support signals, and timely human follow-up.
