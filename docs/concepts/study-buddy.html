Concept 02 · Learning support with people involved

Personalized Study Buddy

Help a learner move forward after a practice mistake while keeping parents, teachers, and advisers involved in encouragement, content guidance, and follow-up.

Recurring practice needTwo limited agent rolesParents and educators remain involved
Working hypothesis for team discussion. The technology supports learning; it does not replace a teacher, detach parents from the learner, diagnose a child, or prove learning impact.
Problem and pain

A learner can remain stuck when timely, appropriate help is unavailable.

A fictional learner answers 52 − 27 as 35, subtracting the smaller digit from the larger one in each place instead of regrouping. A static answer key shows the result but cannot explain the misunderstanding. A general chatbot may simply provide the homework answer. This is a plausible recurring situation, not measured evidence of demand.

Core problem

The learner needs help at the moment of difficulty

Students may struggle outside school hours when a teacher or a parent with subject knowledge is not immediately available.

Pain experienced

Repeated mistakes can become frustration

A general explanation may not address the misunderstanding. Busy parents may want to help but may not know which explanation to try, while teachers cannot provide individual support at every moment.

How the AI could help

Review, teach, check, and adjust within limits

The Learning Review Agent notices a possible learning difficulty from practice attempts. The Teaching Agent selects a teacher-approved hint or activity, presents a new question, and tries another method when the answer check shows that the learner is still struggling.

Why use agents?

The next step depends on what just happened

The system must review an attempt, choose an appropriate method, check whether it worked, adjust within a retry limit, and involve an authorized adult when needed. A fixed quiz cannot complete that learning cycle.

Value for each participant

Student value

Receive a hint matched to the attempt, practice independently, and ask for help without being handed the answer.

Parent value

See a short, authorized progress and support summary, then encourage practice or help arrange follow-up.

Teacher and adviser value

Approve learning activities, guide lesson review, and receive a useful signal when continued difficulty needs human attention.

Proposed agent design

Separate learning review from teaching, then reconnect the learner with people.

The two roles create a visible learning cycle while fixed application rules control correct answers, limits, and who may see the results.

Agent 1

Learning Review Agent

Reviews current and recent practice attempts, records a possible learning difficulty with supporting evidence, and identifies the next practice goal. It does not diagnose or grade the learner.

Agent 2

Teaching Agent

Selects a teacher-approved activity or visual, gives a hint rather than the answer, and presents a different practice question. After an unsuccessful check, it selects one other approved method.

Fixed application rules

The AI does not decide correctness, limits, or sharing.

The application checks every response, including each new practice answer. It also controls retries, session length, allowed content, answer leakage, and what progress information each adult may see.

Human participation

The cycle ends with encouragement or appropriate follow-up.

Parents can encourage the learner and help with study routines. Teachers or advisers approve content and guide lesson review. Continued difficulty produces a support signal, not an automatic diagnosis.

Practice and mock-test mode

Prepare independently, then adjust the next practice plan.

The Study Buddy can create a short quiz or mock test from a teacher-approved question bank. During the attempt, hints and answers stay locked so the result reflects independent practice rather than assisted performance. It works in two ways: as the final check at the end of a practice cycle, as shown in the workflow below, or as a standalone mode the learner starts on their own.

Before the mock test

The learner selects an approved skill and difficulty. The system creates new practice questions without copying a real assessment.

During the attempt

No hints, answer reveals, or coaching. Fixed answer rules record and score questions with clear right or wrong answers after submission.

After submission

The agents explain missed topics using approved material, recommend focused practice, and create a new follow-up quiz. The result is a practice-readiness summary, not an official grade or predicted test score.

Preparation and practice only. The system must not assist during a live, graded, or restricted assessment.

How to read this flow

A practice mistake begins a controlled learning and support cycle.

The agents help choose an explanation and next step. Fixed application rules control correct answers, retry limits, approved content, and who may see the learner’s support summary.

  1. The learner attempts a practice question. The system begins with what the learner actually tried, not with a general request for the homework answer.
  2. Fixed answer rules mark every response. This includes the first attempt and each new practice answer. The AI does not decide whether an answer is correct.
  3. The Learning Review Agent considers where the learner may need help. It records a possible learning difficulty supported by the attempts. It does not diagnose the learner or assign an unofficial grade.
  4. The Teaching Agent selects an approved way to help. It provides a hint or activity and then asks a different practice question, which fixed rules check again.
  5. The system adjusts or involves people. If the new answer is still incorrect, Try Another Method sends the Teaching Agent back with one different approved method. Once the learner answers correctly, a short mock quiz without hints checks whether they can work independently. After the mock quiz, or when the retry limit is reached, the system prepares a short summary for an authorized parent, teacher, or adviser. The adult decides what follow-up is appropriate.
Example seven-minute demo

Show adjustment and participation, not an isolated chatbot.

  1. A fictional student answers 52 − 27 as 35, a common mistake when subtracting two-digit numbers that need regrouping.
  2. Fixed answer rules mark the attempt incorrect. The Learning Review Agent records a possible regrouping difficulty and asks how the learner got the 5 in the ones place before labeling it.
  3. The Teaching Agent selects a teacher-approved place-value activity (trade one ten for ten ones) and presents a new check question: 61 − 38.
  4. The student answers 37, so the fixed rules mark it incorrect. Try Another Method sends the Teaching Agent back to a number-line approach (count up from 38: +2 to 40, +20 to 60, +1 to 61, so 23) within the one-retry limit.
  5. The student answers correctly, then takes a short mock quiz without hints, such as 43 − 19, 70 − 26, 85 − 47, and 58 − 23 (which needs no regrouping). After submission, the system produces a short support summary for parent encouragement and educator follow-up.
Research validation and limits

The learning need is documented; this tutor’s learning benefit is not.

Tutoring is an evidence-supported intervention category, but no measured benefit exists yet for this product, its interpretation of learner mistakes, or its two-agent design.

What the evidence supports

J-PAL’s review of experimental PreK–12 tutoring studies reported an overall pooled effect of 0.37 standard deviations. That result belongs to the tutoring programs studied; it is not an expected effect size for an AI tutor.

Why independent checks matter

In a Penn/Wharton experiment with nearly 1,000 high-school mathematics students in Turkey, general GPT improved assisted practice but students performed 17% worse than the control group when AI was removed. A guarded tutor mitigated that harm but did not outperform the control group on the independent exam.

Smallest credible test

Compare one adaptive subtraction-with-regrouping workflow with a fixed vetted explanation. Use different but comparable questions before support, immediately afterward, and later without hints; measure fresh-question accuracy, retention, answer leakage, and appropriate strategy changes.

Finding that would weaken it

Independent performance does not improve over the fixed explanation, the agent repeatedly misreads learner reasoning, or the experience encourages answer dependence.

Design note: An answer such as 52 − 27 = 35 suggests, but does not prove, a regrouping difficulty; it could also be a simple slip. Ask about the learner’s reasoning before labeling a learning difficulty.

Sources: J-PAL, 2020 · Knowledge at Wharton, 2024

Suggested first prototype · not implemented

One skill, a small approved content library, and one support cycle.

One narrow skill

Two-digit subtraction with regrouping, planned mistake examples, fictional student profiles, new check questions, a short mock quiz, and one authorized parent or educator view. The same idea could also apply to a high-school skill such as the distributive property.

Possible tools

LangGraph to manage the steps; MCP for controlled access to approved activities and hints; fixed answer rules; Base44 or a custom screen; Antigravity to help with development.

Measures

Performance on new post-hint and mock-test questions, answer leakage, appropriate method changes, retry limits followed, useful support signals, and timely human follow-up.
