Concept 01 · Family communication and coordination

Family-School Communication Coordinator

Give parents and students one authorized place to review important school information, ask questions, and coordinate follow-up with teachers or advisers.

Recurring communication needTwo bounded agent rolesEducators own official records and guidance
Working hypothesis for team discussion. It is grounded in lived parenting and school-community experience, but it is not a measured need across schools or a claim of school approval.
Problem and pain

School communication can be scattered, reactive, and difficult to verify.

I experienced this struggle as a parent during my children’s primary-school years in the Philippines. I later served for two years as PTA president and then as a school board member, which gave me another view of communication between families and schools. More recently, I observed a parent friend in the United States navigating similar communication for her children. This lived experience motivates the concept, but it does not prove that every school has the same problem.

Core problem

No single reliable coordination point

Parents, students, and educators may not have one authorized place for school information, questions, and follow-up.

Pain experienced

Messages are missed, repeated, or misunderstood

Families may need to piece together assignments, changed deadlines, events, results, and teacher-marked learning needs. Educators may repeatedly answer similar questions or correct conflicting interpretations.

Agentic response

Retrieve evidence, compare it, then coordinate

The Evidence Agent gathers only approved information and records its source and date. The Coordination Agent gives a cited routine answer or prepares a focused request for an educator when information is unclear or sensitive.

Why agents

The request may require judgment across several sources

The workflow must interpret the question, find relevant records, detect missing or conflicting details, and decide whether to answer or hand control to an educator. A basic search box does not complete that loop.

Value for each participant

Parent value

Review authorized updates, results, and areas needing attention, then ask a focused question or coordinate follow-up.

Student value

See upcoming responsibilities and receive consistent support from home and school.

Teacher and adviser value

Share an approved update once, answer clearer questions, and remain responsible for official records and guidance.

What families could access

A coordination hub, not an autonomous student-information system.

The broader vision can support several school-day needs, but every item must come from an authorized school source and follow the school’s privacy and access rules.

Plans and communications

Important notes, assignments, projects, required materials, deadlines, and school events.

Authorized performance information

Teacher-provided scores, tests, exams, grades, and areas marked for attention. The AI does not calculate an unofficial grade or diagnose the student.

Proactive follow-up

A parent can ask what needs attention or prepare a question for the teacher or adviser. The educator reviews sensitive or consequential guidance.

Proposed agent design

Separate information gathering from communication and follow-up.

The division is useful only if each role remains bounded and independently testable.

Agent 1

Evidence Agent

Uses the permitted family and student scope to retrieve approved notes, assignments, projects, events, and educator-provided performance information. It records sources, dates, and missing context.

Example tool: get_authorized_school_updates

Agent 2

Coordination Agent

Uses verified information to answer a routine question, highlight a teacher-marked area needing attention, or prepare a focused follow-up for a teacher or adviser.

Example tool: create_educator_followup

Deterministic application controls

Identity, privacy, and record access are not left to the model.

The application authenticates each person, applies student and record-level permissions, validates source identifiers, and blocks unauthorized or unsupported responses.

Human authority

Educators own official records and consequential guidance.

The agent cannot invent grades, privately assess a student, change a record, or send a teacher message without the required review. Parents and educators remain active participants.

How to read this flow

A family question moves through five understandable stages.

Not every box represents an AI agent. Some steps are controlled by ordinary application code, approved information tools, or a person with authority.

  1. A parent or student asks a school-related question. For example, they may ask about a project deadline, an event, a teacher-provided result, or a topic marked for review.
  2. The application checks identity and permission first. It confirms which student and records the person is allowed to access before any agent receives information.
  3. The Evidence Agent finds the approved facts. It retrieves only permitted school information and keeps the source, date, and missing details visible.
  4. The Coordination Agent chooses the safe next step. It can provide a cited routine answer. If records conflict, information is missing, or judgment is required, it prepares a focused question instead of guessing.
  5. An educator remains the authority. A teacher or adviser reviews sensitive follow-up and provides the official guidance. The family receives a clear next step without the AI changing records or speaking for the educator.
Illustrative seven-minute demo

Show both useful access and responsible human follow-up.

  1. A fictional parent opens the family hub and reviews a project deadline, school event, and teacher-provided test result.
  2. The application verifies the parent’s permitted student and record scope before the Evidence Agent retrieves synthetic school information.
  3. The parent asks which lesson needs attention. The system surfaces only the teacher-marked topic and its source, not an AI diagnosis.
  4. The Coordination Agent prepares a focused follow-up asking the teacher or adviser what review activity is appropriate.
  5. The educator reviews the request and responds; the family receives a clear, authorized next step.
Research validation and limits

The communication problem has support; this product hypothesis still needs testing.

Published evidence supports clearer family information and shows that targeted communication can affect specific outcomes. It does not prove demand for this coordinator, that generative AI is necessary, or that the two-agent design will reduce educator workload.

What the evidence supports

A 2025 Gallup survey of 808 US K–12 parents found that 42% said their school had shared little or nothing about math-learning goals. An IES evaluation involving about 26,000 elementary students found that adaptive parent messages reduced chronic absence, but did not improve reading or mathematics achievement after one school year.

What remains unproven

Existing platforms already answer school questions. The narrower hypothesis is permissioned, student-specific reconciliation across approved sources, visible conflict handling, and an educator clarification loop. The team still needs to show that useful confusion remains after current portals and messages.

Smallest credible test

Plant an older notice that conflicts with a newer authoritative record. Compare the prototype with ordinary portal or message search on correct answers, time, unsupported statements, clarification usefulness, and educator review effort.

Finding that would weaken it

Families already find reliable answers quickly, approved student-scoped access is impractical, or the workflow creates more educator review and data-entry work than it removes.

Sources: Gallup, 2025 · Institute of Education Sciences, 2020

Suggested MVP · not implemented

Keep the broad vision, test one coordination loop.

One narrow scenario

One fictional class with a project update, one teacher-provided result, one marked review need, and one educator follow-up.

Suggested stack

LangGraph for workflow state; narrow MCP tools for synthetic school information; Base44 or a simple custom interface; Antigravity for development.

Measures

Source accuracy, unauthorized-access denials, unsupported inferences blocked, useful follow-up requests, and educator review time.

Questions for the team
