Concept 01 · Family communication and coordination

Family-School Communication Coordinator

Give parents and students one authorized place to review important school information, ask questions, and coordinate follow-up with teachers or advisers.

Recurring communication needTwo limited agent rolesEducators own official records and guidance
Working hypothesis for team discussion. It is grounded in lived parenting and school-community experience, but it is not a measured need across schools or a claim of school approval.
View the UI concept
Problem and pain

School communication can be scattered, reactive, and difficult to verify.

I experienced this struggle as a parent during my children’s primary-school years in the Philippines. I later served for two years as PTA president and then as a school board member, which gave me another view of communication between families and schools. More recently, I observed a parent friend in the United States managing similar communication for her children. This lived experience motivates the concept, but it does not prove that every school has the same problem.

Core problem

No single reliable place for questions and follow-up

Parents, students, and educators may not have one authorized place for school information, questions, and follow-up.

Pain experienced

Messages are missed, repeated, or misunderstood

Families may need to piece together assignments, changed deadlines, events, results, and learning needs marked by teachers. Educators may repeatedly answer similar questions or correct conflicting information.

How the AI could help

Find approved information, compare it, then coordinate

The Evidence Agent gathers only approved information and records its source and date. The Coordination Agent gives an answer supported by those sources or prepares a focused question for an educator when information is unclear or sensitive.

Why use agents?

The question may require checking several sources

The system must understand the question, find relevant records, notice missing or conflicting details, and decide whether to answer or ask an educator. A basic search box does not complete that cycle.

Opportunity hypothesis: Schools are already willing to use technology for communication. The unmet need may be a more reliable and coordinated approach that fits the school’s actual process, rather than another disconnected app. This still needs direct testing with schools.

Value for each participant

Parent value

Review authorized updates, results, and areas needing attention, then ask a focused question or coordinate follow-up.

Student value

See upcoming responsibilities and receive consistent support from home and school.

Teacher and adviser value

Share an approved update once, answer clearer questions, and remain responsible for official records and guidance.

What families could access

A coordination hub, not an autonomous student-information system.

The broader vision can support several school-day needs, but every item must come from an authorized school source and follow the school’s privacy and access rules.

Plans and communications

Important notes, assignments, projects, required materials, deadlines, and school events.

Authorized performance information

Teacher-provided scores, tests, exams, grades, and areas marked for attention. The AI does not calculate an unofficial grade or diagnose the student.

Proactive follow-up

A parent can ask what needs attention or prepare a question for the teacher or adviser. The educator reviews sensitive or consequential guidance.

Proposed agent design

Separate information gathering from communication and follow-up.

Two roles are useful only if each has a clear job and can be tested separately.

Agent 1

Evidence Agent

Finds approved notes, assignments, projects, events, and teacher-provided performance information that the family is allowed to see. It records sources, dates, and missing details.

Agent 2

Coordination Agent

Uses verified information to answer a routine question, highlight an area marked by a teacher, or prepare a focused follow-up for a teacher or adviser.

Fixed application rules

The AI does not decide identity, privacy, or record access.

The application confirms each person’s identity, checks which student records they may view, verifies the sources, and blocks answers that are unauthorized or unsupported.

Human authority

Educators own official records and important guidance.

The agent cannot invent grades, privately assess a student, change a record, or send a teacher message without the required review. Parents and educators remain active participants.

How to read this flow

A family question moves through five clear stages.

Not every box represents an AI agent. Some steps use fixed application rules, approved information sources, or a person with authority.

  1. A parent or student asks a school-related question. For example, they may ask about a project deadline, an event, a teacher-provided result, or a topic marked for review.
  2. The application checks identity and permission first. It confirms which student records the person may view before any agent receives information.
  3. The Evidence Agent finds the approved facts. It retrieves only permitted school information and keeps the source, date, and missing details visible.
  4. The Coordination Agent chooses the safe next step. It can provide an answer supported by sources. If records conflict, information is missing, or judgment is required, it prepares a focused question instead of guessing.
  5. An educator remains the authority. A teacher or adviser reviews sensitive follow-up and provides the official guidance. The reply returns to the Coordination Agent as the authoritative source, and the answer passes a final privacy and source check before the family receives a clear next step. The AI does not change records or speak for the educator.
Example seven-minute demo

Show both useful access and responsible human follow-up.

  1. A fictional parent opens the family hub and reviews a project deadline, school event, and teacher-provided test result.
  2. The application verifies which student records the parent may view before the Evidence Agent retrieves fictional school information.
  3. The parent asks which lesson needs attention. The system shows only the teacher-marked topic and its source, not an AI diagnosis.
  4. The Coordination Agent prepares a focused follow-up asking the teacher or adviser what review activity is appropriate.
  5. The educator reviews the request and responds; the family receives a clear, authorized next step.
Research validation and limits

Schools are using communication technology, but fragmentation remains a credible problem.

The evidence suggests willingness to use digital tools alongside problems with too many disconnected systems. It does not yet prove that schools are actively seeking this specific platform or that two AI agents would reduce their workload.

Digital communication helps but can create friction

A 2026 peer-reviewed review combined 51 studies of parents’ experiences with technology-based school communication. Parents valued speed and convenience but also reported overcommunication, access and cultural barriers, and privacy concerns.

Schools already use many tools

In a 2025 North American survey, 54% of 58 curriculum leaders reported that their schools used 10 to 15 officially approved education apps. Teachers and parents reported more difficulty with the multi-app environment than administrators expected.

Communication is part of teacher workload

OECD's TALIS 2024 results show that full-time lower-secondary teachers spend an average of 1.8 hours per week communicating and cooperating with parents or guardians.

What remains unproven

The opportunity may be a coordinated communication layer that fits the school’s process, not another standalone app. We still need to confirm that administrators, teachers, families, and students want this approach and that it reduces rather than adds work.

Smallest credible test

Use an older notice that conflicts with a newer official record. Compare the prototype with ordinary portal or message search on correct answers, time, unsupported statements, usefulness of follow-up questions, and educator review effort.

Evidence caution

The app-overload survey directly supports fragmentation, but it had a modest sample and was funded with platform vendor Edsby. We use it as supporting evidence, not proof of market demand.

Sources: Review of Education, 2026 · Cornerstone and Edsby, 2025 · OECD TALIS 2024 · Gallup, 2025 · Institute of Education Sciences, 2020

One narrow scenario

One fictional class with a project update, one teacher-provided result, one marked review need, and one educator follow-up.

Possible tools

LangGraph to manage the steps; MCP to provide controlled access to fictional school information; Base44 or a simple custom screen; Antigravity to help with development.

Measures

Source accuracy, blocked unauthorized access, unsupported statements stopped, useful follow-up requests, and educator review time.
