Two proposals for team consideration

Recurring education needs, designed as bounded agent workflows.

One concept gives families a clearer connection to school information and educator follow-up. The other helps a learner move forward after a mistake while keeping parents and educators involved. Technology supports the relationship; it does not replace it.

Origin of the Family-School hypothesis

Grounded in lived parenting and school-community experience.

This concept is informed by my own experience as a parent navigating school communication during my children’s primary-school years in the Philippines. I later served for two years as PTA president and then as a school board member.

My current observation of a parent friend in the United States managing school communication for her children brought the same recurring pain back into view. These experiences provide a credible starting point, but they do not replace current stakeholder interviews or validate the need across US primary schools.

Compare the directions

Start with the problem, then examine whether agents are justified.

Each concept begins with a recurring human pain and proposes a bounded response. They are alternatives, not parts of one oversized school platform.

01
Family communication

Family-School Communication Coordinator

The problem

School information can be scattered across notebook entries, texts, emails, and separate announcements. Families may miss changes or struggle to know which source is current.

The pain: Parents piece together assignments, events, results, and follow-up needs while educators answer repeated questions or correct misunderstandings.

Proposed response: Retrieve only approved evidence, compare sources and dates, then provide a cited answer or prepare an educator clarification request.

02
Adaptive learning support

Personalized Study Buddy

The problem

A learner can remain stuck when timely, appropriate help is unavailable. A static answer key may show the result without addressing the misunderstanding.

The pain: Repeated mistakes become frustration, busy parents may not know which explanation to try, and teachers cannot provide individual help at every moment.

Proposed response: Interpret the practice attempt, select a vetted hint, check a fresh question, adapt within limits, and involve an authorized adult when needed.

Shared design principle

Agents interpret and adapt. Code controls the rules.

The proposed designs use multiple agents only where responsibilities are meaningfully different.

LangGraph

Suggested orchestration layer for explicit state, agent routing, tool calls, retry limits, and human checkpoints.

Base44 + Antigravity

Base44 is an optional interface layer if integration is viable. Antigravity supports development; it is not a runtime agent.

Participation by design

Parents, students, teachers, and advisers remain visible participants. Narrow tools expose approved synthetic data, and the eventual application receives the required ArgosX security scan.
